# Replying to leads — the playbook

Nov 15 2026 audit: 8 project leads (Nov 3–14) were each emailed a reply. **None answered.**
Every email was delivered, and the reply address works (tested), so nothing was lost.
Here's what went wrong, and the rules that replace it. Studio → Leads drafts messages
to these rules (`src/pages/studio/leadDraft.ts`); use the same rules when writing a reply by hand.

## What went wrong
- **Email only.** We had mobile numbers and never used them. 65% of Filipinos would rather
  reach a business by chat than email or phone (Meta/BCG).
- **Homework.** Every reply ended with 3–5 numbered questions, plus photos. Answering takes
  real work, so it waits, and then it never happens.
- **Everything at once.** Two packages, the price, the deposit, the domain upsell and a timeline,
  all in the first message (105–190 words).
- **A subject that looks automatic.** "Re: We got your RSVP". They never RSVP'd, so it reads as a
  system notice, not a person.
- **Mixed signals.** On the same days, the automatic emails told DIY sign-ups "Your website is
  waiting" and "Need a hand?", pointing them back to the free builder right after we quoted ₱3,000+.

## The rules
1. **Chat first, same day.** If they gave a mobile number: Viber, then SMS. If they only gave an
   Instagram handle: Instagram DM. Email as well, or instead when there's no number.
2. **Who we are, then one detail of theirs.** "Hi Earl, this is Francis from The RSVP Studio."
   Then one thing from their form: the celebrant, the date, the milestone line, the design they picked.
3. **Price in one line, only for Semi-Custom.** "It's ₱3,000, with RSVPs for all 100 guests included."
   For weddings and Custom, don't open with a price. Offer a first look or a call.
4. **End on one easy question, ideally a yes.** "Want me to send a quick preview of the Garden
   design with her name on it?" Never a numbered list in the first message.
5. **Do the first bit of work for them.** Offer a preview with their name on it. **Only offer it if
   someone can make it the same day.**
6. **Ask for details after the yes.** Photos, venue, program and the RSVP deadline come in the
   second or third message, a couple at a time.
7. **Short.** Chat: 2–4 sentences. Email: under 80 words, no lists.
8. **Our voice.** Calm and plain. No exclamation marks, no "Dear valued client", no hype.
9. **Urgent dates.** If the event is under two weeks away, say we can still make it, and when.
   Semi-Custom takes 5–7 business days; rush (+30%) takes 3.
10. **Follow up in chat too.** If there's no answer in 2 days, send one short nudge in the same app.
    Then stop.

## Examples
Birthday, Semi-Custom, chat:
> Hi Cassandra, this is Francis from The RSVP Studio. I got your inquiry about your 18th on
> December 5. "Chapter 18 and becoming" would look great on the Garden design. It's ₱3,000, with RSVPs
> for all 100 guests included. Want me to send you a quick preview with your name on it?

Wedding, chat:
> Hi Sean, this is Francis from The RSVP Studio, following up on the wedding website for you and
> Kyle. I can send a first look of a design for May 22, or we can do a quick call. Which is easier
> for you?

Sources: Meta/BCG via Philstar (chat preference), WeddingPro "8 things you're doing wrong in your lead
replies" (one question; don't lead with a pricing dump), Wedinspire (follow-ups win bookings).
